⚙ The Physics — Why the Pump Fails

Gas Void Fraction (GVF) exceeds ~70% of pump stage volume. The pump stages spin through gas rather than fluid and unload: Pump Intake Pressure (PIP) collapses and motor current (Amps) drops simultaneously. If uncorrected for >25 minutes, the motor runs dry, overheats, and winding insulation fails (Class H insulation threshold: 284°F per API RP 11S).

🔎 Why Engineers Miss It In Time

An experienced engineer manually cross-correlates PIP decline with motor current decline. If both drop in sync, it's gas lock — not mechanical wear. But:

  • Requires manually pulling trend plots from the SCADA historian
  • Only happens if someone notices PIP is drifting low first
  • By the time SCADA fires a low-PIP alarm, the Point of No Return may have passed
  • One production engineer manages 300–500 wells — this correlation cannot happen for every well continuously
⚡ How GDC Improves Detection

  • XGBoost detects the correlated 4-sensor signature (falling PIP, falling amps, stable temp, stable vibration) at 94% confidence — before any single sensor crosses an alarm threshold
  • Lead time: 25 minutes before SCADA would alarm

Asset: ESP-ALPHA-3  ·  Physics: Flowline slug flow causes hydraulic tubing vibration (1.1→2.4 mm/s). Motor temp nominal. AI Confidence: 52%.  ·  SCADA trip limit: 5.0 mm/s — not triggered  ·  Correct action: Surface choke adjustment, NOT a $150k well pull

⚙ The Physics — Why the Pump Looks Broken

High Gas-Liquid Ratio (GLR) causes intermittent gas pockets and liquid slugs to cycle through the surface flowline. Heavy liquid slugs impacting the wellhead piping transmit mechanical shocks down the tubing string, vibrating the downhole sensor (1.1 → 2.4 mm/s rise). The downhole pump is completely healthy. The fault is entirely at the surface choke manifold.

🔎 Why the Default Action Is a $150k Well Pull

When vibration alarms fire in a SCADA environment, the default assumption is downhole mechanical wear. Distinguishing slug flow from bearing damage requires thermal correlation:

  • Slug flow: vibration rises + motor temperature stays flat (healthy pump)
  • Bearing wear: vibration rises + motor temperature climbs (friction)
  • This data exists in 3–5 separate systems — SCADA, downhole portal, lab reports, shift notes
  • Lease operators are trained to be conservative — vibration alarm = shut in and order pull
  • Even senior engineers do this correlation after SCADA alarms — GDC does it 12–24h earlier
⚡ How GDC Distinguishes Surface vs Downhole
  • XGBoost correlates vibration rise vs. winding temperature stability in real-time across all wells
  • 52% confidence "slug_flow" (ambiguous, surface issue) vs high-confidence downhole fault
  • Gemma retrieves OEM troubleshooting guide: "Vibration drift without motor temperature elevation indicates surface flowline slugging"
  • Synthesized assessment: Do NOT pull. Dispatch surface technician to adjust choke valve.

⚙ The Physics — The VFD Tradeoff

Running an ESP at higher VFD frequency (Hz) increases flow rate and revenue. However, motor winding temperature increases non-linearly with frequency — at high frequencies, excess heat degrades Class H polyimide insulation exponentially. Once winding temperature exceeds 270°F consistently, RUL drops from years to weeks. Burnout costs $150,000 in capital + $45,000/day in deferred production.

🔎 Why Manual Optimization Fails
  • Engineers set a conservative, static VFD setpoint (48–52Hz) to stay well within thermal limits
  • Manual "Hz trials" require 48-hour stabilization periods each — weeks of testing per well
  • Manually correlating flow rate gains against temperature trends requires lab data, not just SCADA
  • The high oil-price window may last only days — the optimization window closes before analysis completes
⚡ How GDC Vizier Solves This
  • XGBoost RUL model projects thermal degradation trajectories at each hypothetical Hz setpoint (milliseconds vs 48-hour field trials)
  • OEM Class H insulation limit (284°F per API RP 11S) retrieved from AlloyDB pgvector and used as hard constraint
  • Google Vertex AI Vizier executes 15 Gaussian Process Bandit trials — each Hz is suggested by Vizier's Bayesian model, evaluated against the local XGBoost RUL projection, and reported back to Vertex AI. The edge cluster runs the physics; Vertex AI Vizier drives the search.
  • Converges on the Pareto Frontier — the maximum Hz that maximizes net cash flow without triggering motor burnout

Data Ingestion — Regional Field Network to Edge Bus

High-frequency telemetry from 38 ESP wells across 6 pads flows over the local field-mesh network to the RabbitMQ edge broker every 5 seconds, completely bypassing the slow WAN backhaul to central SCADA.

✕ Close
📡 Sensor Physics
  • Pump Intake Pressure (PIP) — Pump inlet pressure. Drop below 180 PSI indicates gas lock or low reservoir pressure (though positive reservoir pressure must be maintained to prevent pump cavitation). Nominal: 220–280 PSI.
  • Winding Temp °F — Motor insulation health. Class H limit: 284°F. Gas lock causes heat buildup as the motor runs dry.
  • Vibration mm/s — Impeller/bearing wear indicator. Nominal: <0.05 mm/s. Failure threshold: >0.50 mm/s (ISO 10816-3).
  • Motor Amps — VFD current draw. Rising trend = increasing mechanical resistance (sand ingress, scale buildup).
All 4 sensors are correlated simultaneously by the XGBoost model. No single sensor in isolation is sufficient to detect faults early — it's the pattern across all four that matters.
🔌 Why RabbitMQ?
  • Durable queues survive pod restarts without data loss — critical for unattended well-pad operations
  • Topic exchange allows multiple consumers (ML inference, SCADA historian, Grafana) from the same message stream
  • Tested at 12 messages/second sustained throughput per pod on this cluster
📶 Local Field Network vs. WAN
  • The 5s telemetry stream is pulled over a local high-speed field-mesh network within the pad facility — no WAN link involved in data acquisition
  • Central SCADA in headquarters polls over a VSAT/LTE backhaul — typically receiving 15-minute averages only due to bandwidth constraints
  • GDC protects the WAN link by transmitting only insights (kilobytes), not raw telemetry (gigabytes)

ML Anomaly Detection — XGBoost Health Scoring

A trained XGBoost model ingests 6 input features (4 sensor readings + 2 rolling rate-of-change slopes) and outputs a Health Score from 1.0 (nominal) to 0.0 (failure). This model operates purely on numerical sensor data — no documents.

✕ Close
🤖 How XGBoost Works Here
  • Trained on 10,000+ labelled fault signatures per asset class
  • Gradient-boosted decision trees — deterministic, explainable, not a neural network black-box
  • One model per asset class: esp_health.ubj, gas_lift_health.ubj, etc.
  • Health Score of 0.34 = 66% degraded. SCADA single-threshold alarm not triggered yet.
  • Key: SCADA uses absolute thresholds. XGBoost uses correlated trends across all 4 sensors simultaneously.
PSI at 185 is "normal" in isolation. PSI declining at 2.4 PSI/min while temp and amps rise simultaneously is a gas_lock signature. XGBoost detects the pattern; SCADA cannot.
📐 RUL Calculation
  • Health Score is converted to RUL via fault-physics degradation curves calibrated per fault type
  • Exponential weighted average (EWA) over 10 readings smooths out sensor noise
  • Base RUL = time until Health Score would reach the SCADA alarm threshold (0.15)
  • This Base RUL is then refined by the Edge LLM in Pane 5 using retrieved context

AI Reasoning — Gemma 4 8B Edge LLM

The assembled context (sensor data + ML prediction + retrieved documents) is submitted to Gemma 4 8B, running on an NVIDIA L4 GPU within the edge cluster, with no internet dependency.

✕ Close
💡 Why Edge LLM Matters
  • Cloud LLM APIs (Vertex AI, OpenAI) require a WAN connection and introduce 2–5 second network latency — assuming the link is up
  • A wellsite with 4G failover may have packet loss during a storm — exactly when you need the AI most
  • Sensitive operational data (well test reports, production rates, lab results) never leaves the E-House premises
Gemma 4 8B on the L4 GPU achieves comparable reasoning quality to Gemini Flash for structured O&G fault diagnosis, with zero cloud dependency and full offline operation.
🎯 What the LLM Actually Does
  • It is not doing ML inference — the XGBoost model does that in Pane 3
  • It synthesizes structured sensor data with unstructured text documents to produce a human-readable diagnosis and action recommendation
  • It adjusts the Base RUL when retrieved context reveals conditions the ML model's training data didn't capture (e.g., accelerating free-gas trend from the lab report)
  • The recommendation is Gemma's output — not a hardcoded rule

Operations Interface — Human-in-the-Loop & ROI

The AI recommendation is presented to the operator with full financial context. The human retains final control — but now has expert-level intelligence to inform every decision.

✕ Close
🤝 Human-in-the-Loop Design
  • The AI recommends — the operator decides. There is no automated actuation without human approval.
  • Three tiered options give the operator financial and time trade-offs before they commit
  • Every approved action is audit-logged to the fault_sessions table in AlloyDB for compliance and ROI tracking
HITL is not just a safety feature — it's what makes the ROI case credible. The financial ledger records what the operator chose, and what it saved. This is a provable, reproducible financial outcome.
💵 The ROI Equation
  • Without GDC: SCADA alarms when the motor is already at risk. Emergency pull: $150,000 + 48hr downtime at $45k/day = $366,000
  • With GDC: Operator adjusts VFD 14 minutes early. Motor preserved. Cost: $0
  • Annualised ROI across 38-well field at current fault frequency: $7.8M+
