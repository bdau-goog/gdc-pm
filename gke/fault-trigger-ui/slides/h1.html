DECISION SUPPORT

Motor Burnout vs. Sand Bridging

An unloading well forces a blind trade-off: slow down to clear gas and risk sand settling, or shut in and risk sand fallback. Neither is safe without knowing the well's completion history.

VFD SPEED-DOWN 52 → 44 Hz
Reduces pump intake rate. Well stays online if gas is the cause.
✔
Gas Entrainment — CORRECT ACTION
Gas vents up the annulus. Pump recovers hydraulic load. Well stays on production at reduced rate. Zero incremental cost.
✔
Fluid Drawdown + Light Sand — ACCEPTABLE
Safe only above minimum lift velocity and if sand cut confirmed low from completion record.
✘
Fluid Drawdown + Moderate Sand — DANGEROUS
Below ~48 Hz, tubing velocity drops under critical sand-transport limit. Sand settles and bridges the string. Pump seizes on restart.
A zero-dollar VFD command triggers a six-figure workover if sand is present and unaccounted for.
EMERGENCY SHUT-IN Stop Pump
Stops pump completely. Eliminates immediate motor burnout risk — at a cost.
✘
Gas Entrainment — OVERKILL
Unnecessary production deferral and restart risk (electrical inrush, hydraulic hammer). The asset was never in danger.
✔
Fluid Drawdown + Low Sand — LAST RESORT
Acceptable only when completion record confirms low sand cut and standing valve integrity. Defers production.
✘
Fluid Drawdown + Sand Present — DANGEROUS
Stopping flow drops velocity to zero. Suspended sand falls back onto pump assembly. Sand-bridge seizure. Six-figure workover pull.
Shut-in is not a conservative default — without sand context, it can be as damaging as the wrong trim.
To make the safe call, the operator needs to know the sand history of the well.
SCADA cannot provide it. The answer is in the completion record — a document, not a sensor.
